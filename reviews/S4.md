## Verdict

**Approved.** The model follows SPEC 5.6, uses no evaluation seeds, and the full pipeline (300-episode data collection and training) reproduces from scratch at head with **bit-identical weights, training curve and metrics**. The held-out 1-step queue MAE beats persistence by 69.4%, well above the 10% requirement.

2. Full reproduction from scratch (`data/wm/` was empty in this container; it is gitignored):

   ```
   python3.13 -c "import train_world_model as t; t.MODELS='$SCRATCH/s4'; t.train()"
   ```
   (`MODELS` redirected so the committed files were not overwritten.) Results compared with the committed files:
   - `wm_training_curve.csv`: identical (`cmp`), early stop after 100 epochs, best epoch 80.
   - `world_model.pt`: every tensor of the state dict equal (`torch.equal`).
   - `wm_metrics.json`: `test`, `validation` and `n_windows` identical. Test (seeds 215-219, 11116 windows): 1-step queue MAE 1.598 vs persistence 5.225 (improvement 0.694); 3-step 2.048 vs 5.161; 1-step pedestrians 0.643 vs 1.190; departures 0.876 vs 4.746.
   Before the full collection finished, I also evaluated the committed model on the first 20 test-seed episodes only: 1-step queue MAE 1.56 vs persistence 5.68 (72.5% better), consistent with the full test set.
3. Seed hygiene: data seeds are tuning 100-104 plus 200-219 (`africot/wm_data.py:18`); `collect_episode` raises on evaluation seeds (`wm_data.py:55`) and `load_sequences` asserts it again (`train_world_model.py:67`). Splits by seed: train 100-104 and 200-209, validation 210-214 (early stopping), test 215-219 (`train_world_model.py:33-35`). No window crosses episodes.
4. Code read against SPEC 5.6:
   - Observation: noisy perception per approach (queue, 4 class counts, halting danfos, occupancy, departures), waiting pedestrians per crossing, movement queues, phase one-hot, time in phase, normalised time (`africot/world_model.py:33-45`). Action: phase one-hot and green / 60 s.
   - MLP encoder to a 32-d latent, `GRUCell` dynamics on the action, MLP decoder to next-decision queues, pedestrians and departures; multi-step rollout (`world_model.py:66-100`).
   - Data from fixed_time, count_timer, max_pressure and a seeded random-timing controller, through the shield (`wm_data.py:19,26-40,59-67`). Target of decision i is the observation at decision i+1, which is the state after the action applied at i (`wm_data.py:68-75`).
   - CPU, MSE on normalised targets averaged over a 3-step rollout, Adam, early stopping with patience 20 on the validation split; weights, CSV curve, PNG curve and metrics saved (`train_world_model.py:95-160`).
   - The docstring says "in the style of DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 (it is neither)", as the spec requires.
5. Deviations: outage omitted from the data (it would duplicate market exactly for controllers without a slow loop; I agree) and noisy targets for both model and persistence (consistent with SPEC 5.2). Both justified.

## Findings

| # | severity | location | finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | info | `train_world_model.py:80-90` | Persistence is a weak baseline here: decisions are 15 to 90 s apart and a green discharges the served queue, so "queues stay the same" is often far off. The 10% check is passed by a wide margin, but the write-up should describe persistence as the minimum bar it is, not as a strong competitor. |
| 2 | info | `africot/world_model.py:11-12` | Errors are measured against noisy observations (DEVIATIONS.md), so the MAE includes perception noise and is not the error against ground truth. The documenter should say so when quoting the numbers. |
| 3 | info | `models/wm_metrics.json` | The model is trained on four controllers' state distributions; the CEM planner queries it on plans none of them would make. The random-timing controller partly covers this. The online 1-step error in the S5 runs (about 0.8 to 0.9 veh) is lower than the offline test error, so there is no sign of a problem. |
