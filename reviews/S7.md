## Findings

| # | severity | location | finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | low | `africot/coordinator.py:77,122-126`, `run_experiment.py:105` | `novelty()` records a `novelty_trigger` event and restarts the 120 s gap even when the same decision is already a scheduled call, so the call is logged as `scheduled`. The `novelty_triggers` metric counts events, not novelty calls: market seed 4 has 4 events but 2 novelty calls, outage seed 4 has 4 events but 3 calls. The STATUS note ("fires in normal, market and outage") uses the event count. Not a wrong behaviour of the controller, but the dissertation should say which count it reports. |
| 2 | low | `africot/coordinator.py`, `africot/subgoal.py:16` | The subgoal field `valid_for_s` is validated but never used: the coordinator calls every 300 s whatever the VLAM sets. SPEC does not define its use, so this is not a spec violation; the write-up should not claim that the VLAM controls how long its subgoal lasts. |
| 3 | info | `results/mock_wiring/s7_integration_check.json` | The novelty trigger also fires in scenarios with no event (normal seeds 3 and 4, market 1, 3 and 4, outage 4). The builder recorded this honestly; it means the trigger is not specific to incidents. |
| 4 | info | `africot/coordinator.py:12` | The slow loop is treated as instantaneous in simulated time (wall-clock latency recorded only). With about 150 s per 3B call on CPU this is an idealisation that the write-up must state. |
