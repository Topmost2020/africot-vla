## Findings

| # | severity | file:line | finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | major (spec violation) | `africot/env.py:97-111`, `DEVIATIONS.md:7` | The incident does not block the right lane. The blocker is inserted with `departPos="free"` and stopped 5 m further on, so on 4 of 5 tuning seeds it stands 5 m from the **upstream** end of the 300 m approach (43 m on seed 103). A stopped vehicle only occupies its own position: traffic passes it in the median lane and changes back into the kerb lane. On seed 100 the kerb lane downstream of the blocker still carries 1271 vehicle-steps in 1300-2000 s, about 60% of the 2163 it carries in `normal`. The DEVIATIONS entry says the lane "is blocked for the length of the approach downstream of it", which is not what the simulation does. This is why incident AWT is not above normal (committed means 42.5 s vs 43.0 s; incident is lower than normal on seeds 100, 102 and 104), so the scenario does not represent the event the dissertation describes. |
| 2 | major (spec violation, integrity) | `africot/scenarios.py:29-30`, `STATUS.md:8` | The market and outage scenarios are gridlocked on the side road under fixed_time. On market seed 100 south-arm vehicles wait a mean 1228 s (max 2493 s) to enter the network, and only 178 of about 600 post-warm-up south departures complete. The committed file shows the same on every market seed: demand minus (throughput + unfinished) is 428 to 489 vehicles, against 100 to 175 for normal. SPEC section 4 asks for demand tuned on tuning seeds so the scenario is "congested but not gridlocked" under fixed_time. STATUS says "Demand kept at spec values (no tuning needed)", which the data does not support. The zero-jam-teleport check passes vacuously because vehicles queued outside the network cannot teleport. |
| 3 | major (integrity, metrics) | `africot/metrics.py:39-83` | ATT and AWT use tripinfo `duration` and `waitingTime`, which exclude `departDelay`, and vehicles never inserted by the end appear nowhere (`unfinished` counts only vehicles that departed). When an approach spills back past its entry, its delay disappears from every metric, so the reported market AWT (about 78 s) hides south-arm waits of over 20 minutes. Any controller that starves the side road would look better, not worse. This affects S1's reported numbers and every later comparison. |
| 4 | minor | `sim/demand.py:15,58-60` | Pedestrian `departPos`/`arrivalPos` assume an edge length of 300 m (`APPROACH_LEN - 15`), but the generated edges are shorter because of the junction. SUMO clamps the position so runs still work; noting it only in case the walk distance matters later. No fix required. |

## Required fixes

1. **Incident (finding 1).** Make the right (kerb) lane of the east approach, `E2C_1`, unusable for demand traffic from 1200 s to 2100 s over the stretch up to the stop line, for example with `traci.lane.setDisallowed` for the demand classes (as SPEC suggests) or a blocker near the stop line combined with that. Add a test that counts no demand vehicles entering the blocked stretch during the window (vehicles already there may clear). Rerun the S1 check and correct the DEVIATIONS entry so it describes what the simulation does.
2. **Market and outage demand (finding 2).** Tune the market side-road demand (or other demand values allowed by SPEC section 4) on tuning seeds 100-104 only, so the south arm is congested but its insertion backlog does not grow without bound under fixed_time. Record the final values and the reason in `DEVIATIONS.md`, and add a check (in `sim/check_scenarios.py` and a test) on insertion backlog, for example mean departDelay per arm and vehicles not inserted at 3900 s, reported in the results file.
3. **Metrics (finding 3).** Include insertion delay in the vehicle delay metrics (for example ATT = duration + departDelay and AWT = waitingTime + departDelay, or report both forms with the inclusive one as primary), and report the number of vehicles not yet inserted at the end. Record the definition in `DEVIATIONS.md` if it departs from a plain reading of SPEC 5.3. Because S2 results were produced with the current metrics, they will need a rerun after this fix; I will flag the same issue in the S2 review.

## Findings

1. **Resolved (finding 1).** The incident now closes `E2C_1` to the demand classes with `traci.lane.setDisallowed` from 1200 to 2100 s (`africot/env.py` `_incident`), the right lane for right-hand traffic. `tests/test_scenarios.py::test_incident_closes_east_kerb_lane_during_window` checks that no new demand vehicle enters it during the window and that it is used again afterwards. The incident now raises fixed_time AWT over normal (65.7 s against 45.7 s), the expected direction.
2. **Resolved (finding 2), with a note for the dissertation (Medium, not blocking).** Market and outage side-road demand is 200 veh/h, tuned on tuning seeds only and recorded in `DEVIATIONS.md` and `africot/scenarios.py:30-32`. The sweep shows the side road is above fixed_time capacity at every value from 225 upward, because market pedestrians on the east and west crossings block the yielding S turns. The consequence is that market side-road demand (200) is now lower than normal (300), and under fixed_time on tuning seeds market AWT is about equal to normal (45.1 s against 45.7 s). Market still differs from normal in mix, pedestrians and main-road behaviour, and on evaluation seeds every baseline has a higher AWT in market than in normal (`results/*__market__*.json`). This is allowed by SPEC section 4 ("tune demand"), but the documenter must state plainly that the market scenario carries less side-road traffic than normal and why; it must not be described as "heavier side-road demand".
3. **Resolved (finding 3).** ATT and AWT include insertion delay, with right-censoring at 3900 s for unfinished and never-inserted vehicles (`africot/metrics.py:61-135`); completed-trip forms kept as `att_completed`/`awt_completed`; `not_inserted` and `depart_delay_by_arm` reported. Unit test `tests/test_baselines.py::test_tripinfo_excludes_warmup_and_counts_insertion_delay` covers each case.
4. **Low.** The 2 `wrong lane` teleports in the incident runs are a side effect of the lane closure (a vehicle that cannot reach its lane in time). They are not jam teleports and are recorded in the results file, so they satisfy the done-when check, but they should be mentioned in the write-up.
