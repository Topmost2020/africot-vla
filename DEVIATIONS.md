| 2026-10-06 | S1 | (Superseded the same day after reviews/S1.md finding 1.) The first version used a broken-down vehicle inserted at a free spot on the kerb lane. It stopped near the upstream end of the approach, traffic passed it and used the kerb lane downstream, so the lane was not blocked. | Replaced by the lane closure in the next row. |
| 2026-10-06 | S1 | Incident: the kerb lane of the east approach (`E2C_1`) is closed to all demand vehicle classes (passenger, bus, motorcycle) with `traci.lane.setDisallowed` from 1200 s to 2100 s, then its permissions are restored. Vehicles already on the lane at 1200 s (3 on seed 100) may clear it; no other demand vehicle enters it during the window (tested). | This is the mechanism SPEC 4 suggests. It blocks the whole lane up to the stop line, so all east-approach traffic shares the median lane. |
| 2026-10-06 | S1 | Scenario event times (incident 1200 to 2100 s, outage 1200 to 2400 s) are read as simulation clock times, which include the 300 s warm-up. | The spec does not say which clock. Both windows fall inside the measured period either way. |
| 2026-10-06 | S1 | The "zero jam teleports" check counts teleports whose SUMO reason is `jam`. Across the 20 tuning runs there were 2 teleports with reason `yield` (market and outage, seed 103, the same okada turning S to W waiting to yield to pedestrians) and 0 with reason `jam`. | A yield teleport is a junction-blocking artefact of the sublane model with dense pedestrian flow, not a congestion jam. Both counts are recorded in `results/s1_scenario_check.json`. |
| 2026-10-06 | S3 | The shield enforces the pedestrian crossing time on every phase that gives green to a crosswalk, not only when pedestrians are observed waiting. | Perception misses each pedestrian with probability 0.05 and adds noise, so a waiting pedestrian can go unseen. Applying the minimum unconditionally is stricter than the spec and never less safe. In practice it only lifts phase C greens below 16 s to 16 s (phase A already has the 10 s minimum, which covers the 10 s south crossing time). |
| 2026-10-06 | S3 | The baseline results from S2 were rerun through the shield and overwritten (`--force`). All reported runs from S3 on pass through the shield. | SPEC 5.4 says the shield applies to max_pressure, and SPEC 1 says it cannot be bypassed, so every controller now goes controller, shield, signals. |
| 2026-10-06 | S1/S2 | ATT and AWT include insertion delay and cover every demand vehicle scheduled to depart after the warm-up: ATT = departDelay + duration, AWT = departDelay + waitingTime. Vehicles still in the network at 3900 s, and vehicles never inserted, are right-censored at 3900 s (a never-inserted vehicle counts 3900 s minus its scheduled departure for both). The completed-trip, in-network-only means are kept as `att_completed` and `awt_completed`, and `not_inserted` and `depart_delay_by_arm` are reported. AQL counts halting demand vehicles only. | reviews/S1.md finding 3: with SUMO's plain tripinfo means, delay of vehicles queued outside the network vanished, so a controller that starved the side road looked better. Censoring makes the AWT a lower bound for vehicles that did not finish, the same rule for every controller. |
| 2026-10-06 | S2 | Perception also returns halting vehicles per movement (`movement_queues`), from each halting vehicle's route, with the same noise as the other counts. | SPEC 5.2 lists per-approach quantities, but max_pressure (SPEC 5.4) needs per-movement queues to tell phase A from phase B; a camera can see turn lanes and indicators. |
