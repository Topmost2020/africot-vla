# Stage S5: CEM planner (africot_noslow and africot_nowm)

## Purpose and place in the architecture

The AfriCoT-VLA design splits control into a slow loop, in which a vision-language-action model proposes a compact subgoal, and a fast loop, in which a latent world model and a planner choose signal timings that minimise a subgoal-defined cost (`SPEC.md`, `docs/design.html`). Stage S5 builds the fast loop planner and two of its three controller variants. The variant with a fixed default subgoal (`africot_noslow`) is the "without slow loop" ablation, and the variant without a learned model (`africot_nowm`) is the "without world model" ablation. The third variant, `africot_full`, is wired in the stage on integration (`docs/stages/S7.md`). Both ablations are intended to isolate the contribution of the fast loop before the slow loop is added.

## What was built

The planner was committed in `2bb5955`, and all 40 result files carry that git hash (`results/africot_noslow__*.json`, `results/africot_nowm__*.json`).

- `africot/planner_cem.py`, containing `CEMPlanner`, the vectorised cost function `_cost_terms`, and a `WaitTracker` that estimates how long each approach or crosswalk has had demand without green from observations alone.
- `tests/test_planner.py`, covering the max-wait penalty, the subgoal weights, a seeded CEM run with greens in range, and the heuristic serving the longer queue.
- `run_experiment.py` entries for the two controllers, which pass every decision through the shield of the stage on the safety shield.

## Exact settings

From `africot/planner_cem.py` and the `config.planner` block of the result files:

- Plans have 3 steps. Each step is a phase drawn from a categorical distribution and a green time drawn from a Gaussian clipped to 10 to 60 s and rounded to whole seconds.
- The Cross-Entropy Method uses 64 samples, 4 iterations and 8 elites. The initial green distribution has mean 30 s and standard deviation 15 s, and the standard deviation is floored at 2 s. The generator is seeded with `[seed, 999]`.
- The cost of an imagined rollout is summed over the 3 steps as the weighted queue, plus the pedestrian weight times predicted pedestrian waiting, plus the transit priority times predicted waiting danfos, plus 1000 times the amount by which the estimated wait exceeds the subgoal maximum wait. Only the first step is returned, and it is then corrected by the shield.
- `africot_noslow` uses the default subgoal of the specification (all queue weights 1.0, pedestrian weight 1.0, maximum wait 120 s, transit priority 1.0, validity 300 s) and the world model in `models/world_model.pt`.
- `africot_nowm` does not load a world model. It scores 3 phases times green times of 10 to 60 s in steps of 5 s for the first step only, using the current queues, an exponentially smoothed arrival-rate estimate (weights 0.7 and 0.3) and fixed discharge rates in veh/s (phase A: west 1.0, east 0.5; phase B: east 1.0; phase C: south 0.5).
- No planner parameter was tuned. Both controllers observe only the noisy perception output.

## How it was tested

The reviewer ran `python3.13 -m pytest -q` at the branch head and recorded 68 passed (`reviews/S5.md`). Both controllers were run on the four scenarios and evaluation seeds 0 to 4, giving 40 runs, and all files record `seed_role` as `evaluation` and `vlam_backend` as null.

Mean average waiting time (AWT, in seconds, with insertion delay included as described in `DEVIATIONS.md`), taken from `results/summary.md`:

| scenario | max_pressure | africot_noslow | africot_nowm |
|---|---|---|---|
| normal | 17.0 | 19.3 | 39.9 |
| market | 25.9 | 23.8 | 48.2 |
| incident | 35.8 | 39.9 | 72.6 |
| outage | 25.9 | 23.8 | 48.2 |

The outage rows equal the market rows because neither variant has a slow loop, so the outage window changes nothing (`results/summary.md`). Relative to max_pressure, `africot_noslow` is about 8 percent lower on market and about 14 percent and 11 percent higher on normal and incident. `africot_nowm` is worse than max_pressure and fixed_time on normal and incident. The planner is therefore not reported as superior to the strongest baseline in this stage. The 40 files record 0 safety violations. Summing `info.shield_corrections_by_rule`, `africot_noslow` received 1418 `ped_crossing` corrections and `africot_nowm` received 433 `ped_crossing` and 112 `max_green` corrections. The `africot_nowm` runs contain one jam teleport and up to 21 vehicles never inserted in a single run (incident, seed 4), and the `africot_noslow` runs contain 3 teleports with reason other than jam or yield and at most 2 vehicles never inserted. The mean online one-step queue error of the world model in `africot_noslow` runs, from `info.wm_1step_queue_mae_mean`, is 1.17 vehicles (range 0.92 to 1.72 over the 20 runs). The builder's note in `STATUS.md` gives about 0.8 vehicles, which does not match the committed files, and the files are used here.

The reviewer reran `africot_noslow` normal seed 0 and `africot_nowm` incident seed 4 at the branch head and found the metrics and shield correction counts identical to the committed files (`reviews/S5.md`).

## Deviations from the specification

None are recorded for this stage in `DEVIATIONS.md`. The reviewer asked that one interpretation be stated, and it is stated here: the specification cost includes waiting danfos, but the world model does not predict danfos (it predicts queues, pedestrian waiting and departures). The planner therefore approximates waiting danfos as the predicted queue multiplied by the currently observed danfo share of the queue on each approach (`africot/planner_cem.py`, `_danfo_share`).

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The stage was approved without required fixes (`reviews/S5.md`). Four non-blocking findings were raised: the danfo approximation above (low, stated in this document); `reset()` does not clear the `africot_nowm` arrival-rate estimate (low, no effect on results because each run builds a new controller); the cost is summed per step and not weighted by step duration (info, stated under limitations); and the discharge rates are fixed engineering estimates (info, stated in the settings). The reviewer and builder notes agree with the files that `africot_noslow` loses to max_pressure on normal and incident.

## Limitations

The cost is summed per plan step, so a long green is not penalised for the time it covers. The discharge rates of `africot_nowm` are untuned guesses, so that variant is an ablation of one particular heuristic and not a bound on what a world-model-free planner can reach. The planner was not tuned on the tuning seeds, and the comparison rests on 5 evaluation seeds with sample standard deviations that are large in some cells (for example 29.1 s for `africot_nowm` on incident in `results/summary.md`). Results do not include any slow-loop effect.

## Dissertation paragraph

The fast loop is designed as a Cross-Entropy Method planner over three-step signal plans, in which a latent world model imagines candidate plans and a subgoal-weighted cost selects the first step, which is always passed through the safety shield. Two ablations were run on five evaluation seeds in four scenarios: a variant with a fixed default subgoal and a variant that replaces the world model with a one-step queue-discharge heuristic. With the default subgoal, the planner recorded a lower mean waiting time than max-pressure control only in the market scenario (23.8 s against 25.9 s) and a higher one in the normal and incident scenarios, and the heuristic variant was weaker than both fixed-time and max-pressure control. These results are intended to be read as a baseline for the contribution of the slow loop, and not as evidence that the planner alone improves on the strongest classical controller (`results/summary.md`).
