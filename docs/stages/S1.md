# Stage S1: Junction and scenarios

## Purpose and place in the architecture

AfriCoT-VLA is designed as a slow loop (a vision-language-action model), a fast loop (a latent world model with a cross-entropy-method planner) and a rule-based safety shield, evaluated on one Lagos-style T-junction in SUMO (see `SPEC.md` and `docs/design.html`). Stage S1 builds the environment in which every later component is evaluated: the junction network, the signal phases, the mixed vehicle fleet, the seeded demand generator and the four scenarios (normal, market, incident and outage). It is intended to give all controllers the same traffic and the same pedestrians for a given scenario and seed, so that differences between controllers are attributable to control and not to the demand.

## What was built

- `sim/vtypes.add.xml`, the four vehicle types.
- `sim/demand.py`, a seeded Poisson generator of vehicles and pedestrians.
- `africot/scenarios.py`, the scenario definitions and the seed sets.
- `africot/signals.py`, the three signal phases built from link indices in the network file, and a conflict checker.
- `africot/env.py`, the TraCI environment (`JunctionEnv`), including amber and all-red insertion and the incident lane closure.
- `africot/metrics.py`, the delay and queue metrics.
- `sim/check_scenarios.py` and `sim/tune_demand.py`, the stage check and the demand tuning script.
- Tests in `tests/test_network.py` and `tests/test_scenarios.py`.

## Exact settings

Network (`sim/junction.edg.xml`). The main road runs east to west with two lanes in each direction at 13.89 m/s (50 km/h). The side road is the south arm with one lane in each direction at 11.11 m/s (40 km/h). Every edge carries a 2.0 m sidewalk lane, and there are three crossings (east, west and south arms). Simulation uses a 1 s step, a sublane lateral resolution of 0.8 m and a teleport time of 300 s (`africot/env.py`).

Vehicle types (`sim/vtypes.add.xml`):

| type | class | length (m) | width (m) | max speed (m/s) | other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| car | passenger | 4.5 | 1.8 | 16.67 | |
| danfo | bus | 5.5 | 2.0 | 13.89 | |
| keke | passenger | 2.7 | 1.4 | 11.11 | minGapLat 0.4 |
| okada | motorcycle | 2.0 | 0.8 | 16.67 | arbitrary lateral alignment, minGapLat 0.1, maxSpeedLat 1.5, lcSublane 2.0 |

Signal phases (`africot/signals.py`). Phase A serves the main road (west through and right, east through, with the east left turn permissive) and the south crosswalk. Phase B gives the east approach a protected left together with east through. Phase C serves the side road with its turns yielding to pedestrians, and gives green to the east and west crosswalks. Amber is 3 s and all-red 2 s on every phase change.

Timing and seeds (`africot/scenarios.py`). Warm-up 300 s, measured period 3600 s, run end 3900 s. Tuning seeds 100 to 104, evaluation seeds 0 to 4, data seeds 200 to 219. Only the tuning seeds are used in S1.

Demand per origin arm in vehicles per hour (`africot/scenarios.py`):

| scenario | west | east | south | mix (car, danfo, keke, okada) | pedestrians per hour | crossing split (east, west, south) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal | 700 | 700 | 300 | 0.45, 0.20, 0.15, 0.20 | 300 | 1/3 each |
| market | 700 | 700 | 200 | 0.30, 0.20, 0.25, 0.25 | 900 | 0.4, 0.4, 0.2 |
| incident | as normal | | | as normal | as normal | as normal |
| outage | as market | | | as market | as market | as market |

Turning shares are 80/20 (west to east or south), 75/25 (east to west or south) and 50/50 (south to east or west). The incident closes the kerb lane of the east approach, `E2C_1`, to demand vehicle classes from 1200 s to 2100 s with `traci.lane.setDisallowed`. The outage window is 1200 s to 2400 s; in S1 the outage scenario carries market demand and has no effect on the signal controllers, because the connectivity window only matters to the slow loop built in a later stage.

Metrics (`africot/metrics.py`). Average travel time (ATT) and average waiting time (AWT) cover every demand vehicle scheduled to depart after the warm-up and include insertion delay. Vehicles still in the network at 3900 s, and vehicles never inserted, are right-censored at 3900 s. The completed-trip forms are kept as `att_completed` and `awt_completed`.

## How it was tested

The builder's final note records 54 tests passing (`STATUS.md`). The reviewer's rerun at the branch head recorded 49 passed in 43 s (`reviews/S1.md`, re-review). The tests check the lanes, speeds and sidewalks, the three crossings, the movement set without U-turns, the absence of conflicting greens in every signal state (including a deliberately unsafe state that the checker must flag), the phase contents, the sublane setting and vehicle types, seeded demand reproducibility, demand rates and turning shares, disjoint tuning and evaluation seeds, amber and all-red insertion during a run, a short run of each scenario, and that no demand vehicle enters `E2C_1` during the incident window while it is used again afterwards.

The stage check ran fixed_time for 3900 s on each scenario and tuning seeds 100 to 104 (`results/s1_scenario_check.json`, code commit `91dca21`). The file reports zero jam teleports in all 20 runs, two teleports in total (both with reason `wrong lane`, in the incident scenario), and at most four vehicles never inserted in any run. Mean AWT over the five tuning seeds was 45.7 s (normal), 45.1 s (market), 65.7 s (incident) and 45.1 s (outage). Okada lateral standard deviation ranged from 0.79 m to 0.90 m across the 20 runs, against 0.25 m to 0.31 m for cars, which is the lane-filtering behaviour the stage was intended to show. The reviewer rebuilt the network from the sources and found it identical, checked the phase states against SUMO's own foe matrix, and reran the incident seed 100 and market seed 103 checks, both matching the committed file field by field (`reviews/S1.md`).

## Deviations from the specification

From `DEVIATIONS.md`:

- Incident: the first implementation used a stopped vehicle as the blocker, which was superseded because it did not block the lane downstream of it. The kerb lane `E2C_1` is now closed to demand classes from 1200 s to 2100 s. Vehicles already on the lane at closure may clear it (3 on seed 100, per the reviewer's check).
- Event times are read as simulation clock times, which include the 300 s warm-up.
- The zero jam teleport check counts teleports with reason `jam`. The tuning runs had two teleports with reason `yield` in an earlier version of the check, and the final file records two teleports with reason `wrong lane`; neither is a jam.
- ATT and AWT include insertion delay and censor unfinished and never-inserted vehicles at 3900 s (details above).
- Market and outage side-road demand is 200 veh/h instead of the specified 550 veh/h. `sim/tune_demand.py` tried 550, 300, 275, 250, 225 and 200 veh/h on seeds 100 to 104 with the criterion that at most five south vehicles are never inserted on every seed and that the mean south insertion delay is below 60 s. Only 200 veh/h met it (`results/demand_tuning.json`: mean south insertion delay 1177.0 s at 550 and 3.3 s at 200; maximum south vehicles never inserted 395 at 550 and 4 at 200).

The market scenario therefore carries less side-road traffic than the normal scenario (200 against 300 veh/h). It must not be described as heavier on the side road. Its congestion comes from the vehicle mix, the 900 pedestrians per hour, and pedestrians crossing the east and west arms, which block the yielding turns from the south.

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The first review requested changes (`reviews/S1.md`): the incident blocker did not block the kerb lane (finding 1), the market and outage side road was gridlocked under fixed_time (finding 2), and the metrics excluded insertion delay and never-inserted vehicles (finding 3). A fourth, minor finding noted that pedestrian departure positions assume a 300 m edge length, with SUMO clamping the value; no fix was required. The re-review approved the stage after all three fixes, noting that the two `wrong lane` teleports in the incident runs are a side effect of the lane closure and must be mentioned, and that the market demand reduction must be stated plainly. Both points are addressed above.

## Limitations

The stage check uses fixed_time only and tuning seeds only, so it describes scenario behaviour and not controller performance. At 200 veh/h the market side road is less loaded than in the normal scenario, so the scenario stresses the junction mainly through pedestrians and the vehicle mix. Under fixed_time, market AWT on tuning seeds (45.1 s) is about equal to normal (45.7 s). The incident closure is a lane permission change and not a physical obstruction, and it produces two `wrong lane` teleports in the check runs. A small number of vehicles (at most four in a run) are never inserted by the end of the run. Pedestrian walk distances are clamped by SUMO.

## Text for the dissertation

The experimental environment is a single Lagos-style T-junction implemented in SUMO. The main road has two lanes in each direction and the side road one, with sidewalks and three signalised crossings. The vehicle fleet is intended to represent informal-transport heterogeneity through four types, namely cars, danfo buses, keke tricycles and okada motorcycles, the last of which is configured with arbitrary lateral alignment and sublane lane-filtering. Demand is generated by a seeded Poisson process, and four scenarios (normal, market, incident and outage) are designed to probe typical, pedestrian-heavy, lane-blocked and connectivity-loss conditions. The side-road demand of the market scenario was reduced from 550 to 200 vehicles per hour on separate tuning seeds, because the specified value produced an insertion backlog that grew without bound under fixed-time control (`results/demand_tuning.json`). Delay metrics are designed to include the time vehicles spend waiting to enter the network, so that a controller cannot appear better by starving an approach. Under fixed-time control on the tuning seeds, mean AWT was 45.7 s, 45.1 s and 65.7 s in the normal, market and incident scenarios respectively (`results/s1_scenario_check.json`).
