# Stage S3: Safety shield

## Purpose and place in the architecture

The AfriCoT-VLA design places a rule-based safety shield between every controller and the traffic signals, so that no proposal from the slow loop, the fast loop or a baseline can reach the signals without being checked (`SPEC.md`, `docs/design.html`). Stage S3 builds this shield and an independent audit that counts rule violations from simulator ground truth. It is intended to support the claim that safety does not depend on the learned components, and to give later stages a mechanism that cannot be switched off.

## What was built

The shield was committed in `5dff6a5`, the first shielded results in `5e69ba4`, and the baselines were rerun through the shield after the S1 and S2 fixes in `c867954` (code commit `91dca21`).

- `africot/shield.py`, containing the `Shield` class and an independent `SafetyAudit`.
- `africot/runner.py`, in which `run_episode` routes every decision through controller, shield and environment. The shield cannot be disabled in this function.
- `africot/env.py` (`apply_phase`), the only code path that changes the signals, which inserts the amber and all-red transition.
- `tests/test_shield.py`, 11 tests.

## Exact settings

Rules, applied to each proposal in this order (`africot/shield.py`):

1. Starvation guard: an approach or crosswalk with observed demand and no green for more than 180 s is served next, longest waiting first.
2. Maximum green of 90 s for a phase in total. An extension that would pass 90 s is cut, and a phase already at 90 s is ended.
3. Minimum green of 10 s for a new phase.
4. Pedestrian minimum: a phase that gives green to a crosswalk lasts at least the crossing time, computed as crossing length divided by 1.2 m/s plus 4 s, rounded up. From the network this gives 16 s for the east and west crossings and 10 s for the south crossing (`results/max_pressure__market__3.json`, `config.shield.crossing_times`).
5. Amber of 3 s and all-red of 2 s on every phase change, inserted by the environment.

Every correction is recorded with its rule and reason. The shield uses only the controller's observation, which is the noisy perception, and its own signal history. The audit uses ground truth to check conflicting greens, transition lengths, minimum and maximum green, the pedestrian minimum when pedestrians are waiting at green start, and a starvation bound of 275 s (180 s threshold plus the longest green and the 5 s transition). Runs use evaluation seeds 0 to 4, three baselines and four scenarios, 60 runs.

## How it was tested

The builder recorded 44 tests passing at the S3 build, and the reviewer recorded 49 passing at the branch head in 43 s (`STATUS.md`, `reviews/S3.md`). The 11 shield tests cover crossing times read from the network, a short green raised to the minimum, a long green capped, an extension cut at the maximum, a phase at the maximum being ended, a pedestrian phase receiving the crossing time, the starvation guard and the resetting of its clock, an invalid phase being corrected, an untouched safe proposal, and two tests showing that the audit flags short greens when the shield is bypassed.

Over the 60 shielded baseline runs in `results/`, the audit recorded 0 safety violations. The shield made 2833 `ped_crossing` corrections, 4 `max_green` corrections and 2 `starvation` corrections in total, summed from `info.shield_corrections_by_rule` in the files. According to the reviewer, the `max_green` and `starvation` corrections all occur in max_pressure runs, where the controller kept reselecting phase A until the shield intervened (`reviews/S3.md`). Mean AWT with the shield, from the same 60 files, is 36.3 s, 38.1 s and 17.0 s for fixed_time, count_timer and max_pressure in the normal scenario, and 42.4 s, 44.1 s and 25.9 s in the market scenario (full table in `docs/stages/S2.md`).

The reviewer reran five shielded runs and found the metrics, correction counts and audit results identical to the committed files. The reviewer also reproduced three of the first S3 results at code commit `5dff6a5` in a separate worktree (`reviews/S3.md`).

## Deviations from the specification

From `DEVIATIONS.md`:

- The pedestrian crossing minimum is applied on every phase that gives green to a crosswalk, not only when pedestrians are observed waiting, because perception can miss a waiting pedestrian. This is stricter than the specification and in practice only lifts phase C greens below 16 s.
- The S2 baseline results were rerun through the shield and overwritten, because the shield applies to max_pressure and cannot be bypassed. All reported runs from S3 onward pass through the shield.

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The reviewer approved the stage with no required fixes (`reviews/S3.md`). Three notes were recorded. First, 59 of 60 files carry the code hash with a `-dirty` suffix, a labelling effect of the grid overwriting tracked result files; the commit exists and reproduces the files. Second, in max_pressure market and outage seed 3 there is one `jam` and two `yield` teleports at about 2900 s, from vehicles held inside the junction while yielding to dense pedestrian flow, with no signal violation found by the audit. Third, the audit checks the pedestrian minimum only when ground-truth pedestrians are waiting at green start, which matches the specification wording, and no unit test injects a wrong amber or all-red length, although the transition check passed in all 60 runs.

## Limitations

The audit and the shield share the same rule constants, so the audit tests that the implementation follows the rules and not that the rules are sufficient for real safety. The shield works on noisy observations and so can act late on starvation; the longest observed no-green interval with demand was 195 s for max_pressure (reviewer's measurement in `reviews/S3.md`), within the audit bound. The pedestrian minimum models crossing time only and does not model drivers' behaviour, and the one jam teleport above shows that sublane yielding artefacts remain outside the shield's reach. Results are limited to five evaluation seeds and to the three baselines, since the learned controllers do not yet exist at this stage.

## Text for the dissertation

A rule-based safety shield is designed to sit between every controller and the signals, so that learned or heuristic proposals are checked before they take effect. It is intended to enforce a minimum green of 10 s, a maximum green of 90 s, a pedestrian crossing time derived from crossing length at 1.2 m/s plus 4 s, a starvation limit of 180 s, and an amber of 3 s followed by an all-red of 2 s on every phase change. Each correction is logged with the rule that triggered it. An independent audit that reads simulator ground truth was used to count violations, and over 60 runs of the three baselines on four scenarios and five evaluation seeds it recorded none (`results/*.json`). The shield made 2833 pedestrian-crossing, 4 maximum-green and 2 starvation corrections in those runs, which is intended to indicate that its rules are exercised but seldom override the baselines apart from pedestrian timing.
