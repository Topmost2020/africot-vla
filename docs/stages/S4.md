# Stage S4: Latent world model

## Purpose and place in the architecture

The AfriCoT-VLA design places a learned world model in the fast loop, where a planner queries it to imagine the consequence of candidate signal plans before the first step of the best plan is passed through the safety shield (`SPEC.md`, `docs/design.html`). Stage S4 builds and trains that model. It is intended to give the planner of the stage on the CEM planner (`docs/stages/S5.md`) a fast learned predictor of queues, waiting pedestrians and departures, so that the planner does not need to call the simulator. The code states plainly that the model is written in the style of DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 but is neither of them (`africot/world_model.py`).

## What was built

The model, data collection and training script were committed in `2e7f7ad`, and the trained weights, curve and metrics in `c16d7c0` (`models/wm_metrics.json` records `git_hash` `2e7f7ad`).

- `africot/world_model.py`, containing `LatentWorldModel`: a multilayer-perceptron encoder from the 38-dimensional perception vector to a 32-dimensional latent, a `GRUCell` that advances the latent given the action, and a multilayer-perceptron decoder to nine targets (queue per approach, waiting pedestrians per crossing and departures per approach). It supports multi-step rollout for planning.
- `africot/wm_data.py`, which logs per-decision observations, applied actions and next-decision targets from four controllers through the shield.
- `train_world_model.py`, which collects episodes and trains the model.
- `tests/test_world_model.py`, covering the feature layout, rollout shapes, save and load, refusal of evaluation seeds, and disjoint split seeds.
- Outputs: `models/world_model.pt`, `models/wm_metrics.json`, `models/wm_training_curve.csv` and `models/wm_training_curve.png`.

## Exact settings

From `models/wm_metrics.json` and `africot/world_model.py`:

- Inputs and targets both come from the noisy perception output, so the model sees only what the deployed controller sees. The observation holds, per approach, queue, four class counts, halting danfos, occupancy and departures, then waiting pedestrians per crossing, movement queues, the phase as one-hot, time in phase and normalised time. The action holds the phase one-hot and the green time divided by 60 s.
- Latent size 32, hidden size 64, Adam with learning rate 0.001, batch size 256, a maximum of 300 epochs and early stopping with patience 20 on the validation split. The loss is the mean squared error on normalised targets, averaged over a 3-step rollout. The torch seed is 0 and training runs on the CPU.
- Data are 300 episodes from four controllers (`fixed_time`, `count_timer`, `max_pressure` and a seeded random-timing controller with a random phase and a green of 10 to 60 s) in the normal, market and incident scenarios, on tuning seeds 100 to 104 and data seeds 200 to 219.
- The split is by seed. Training uses seeds 100 to 104 and 200 to 209, validation uses 210 to 214 and testing uses 215 to 219. The windows number 32906, 11027 and 11116 respectively. Evaluation seeds 0 to 4 are refused in the collection code and checked again when the sequences are loaded.
- Training stopped after 100 epochs, with the best validation loss of 0.1967 at epoch 80 and a recorded training wall time of 44.1 s (`models/wm_metrics.json`).

## How it was tested

The reviewer ran `python3.13 -m pytest -q` at the branch head and recorded 68 passed (`reviews/S4.md`). The reviewer also collected the 300 episodes from scratch and retrained, and found the training curve identical, every tensor of the state dict equal, and the test, validation and window-count results identical to the committed files.

On the held-out test seeds 215 to 219 (11116 windows), the mean absolute error (MAE) of the model against the persistence baseline (`models/wm_metrics.json`):

| target | horizon | model | persistence |
|---|---|---|---|
| queue (vehicles) | 1 step | 1.60 | 5.23 |
| queue (vehicles) | 2 steps | 1.84 | 5.72 |
| queue (vehicles) | 3 steps | 2.05 | 5.16 |
| waiting pedestrians | 1 step | 0.64 | 1.19 |
| departures | 1 step | 0.88 | 4.75 |

The one-step queue error is 69.4 percent lower than persistence, which passes the specified check of at least 10 percent (`check_1step_beats_persistence_by_10pct` is true in the metrics file). The improvement is 67.8 percent at two steps and 60.3 percent at three steps. Validation errors are close to the test errors (one-step queue MAE 1.53 against 5.18 for persistence). Per-target errors are listed in the same file, with the largest one-step error for the south queue (1.86).

## Deviations from the specification

Two entries in `DEVIATIONS.md` apply to this stage.

1. The world-model data cover the normal, market and incident scenarios and not the outage scenario. Outage differs from market only in the connectivity window of the slow loop, the four data controllers have no slow loop, and outage episodes would therefore duplicate market episodes exactly.
2. Inputs and targets are both taken from noisy perception, and the persistence baseline uses the noisy current queue. This follows the specification rule that ground truth is not used for control, and it means that errors are measured against noisy observations for both the model and persistence.

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The stage was approved without required fixes (`reviews/S4.md`). Three informational findings were raised and are carried into this document. Persistence is a weak baseline, because decisions are 15 to 90 s apart and a green discharges the served queue, so the 10 percent check should be read as a minimum bar. The MAE includes perception noise, as stated above. The model is trained on the state distribution of four controllers, whereas the planner queries it on plans none of them would produce, which the random-timing controller only partly covers. The reviewer noted that the online one-step error in the planner runs did not suggest a problem, and the stage on the CEM planner reports 1.17 vehicles from the committed result files (`docs/stages/S5.md`).

## Limitations

The reported errors are against noisy observations and not against ground truth. The comparison baseline is persistence only, and no stronger predictor (for example a per-controller queue-discharge model) was compared. The model is small and was trained once with a single torch seed, so no variability across training seeds is available. Because outage episodes were omitted, the model has not seen data from the outage window, although the fast loop is unchanged in that window. The data come from a single junction geometry in simulation.

## Dissertation paragraph

The fast loop is designed around a small latent world model that is intended to support planning without repeated calls to the simulator. An encoder maps the noisy perception vector to a 32-dimensional latent, a gated recurrent cell advances it given the signal action, and a decoder predicts queues, waiting pedestrians and departures at the next decision. The model was trained on 300 simulated episodes from four controllers with a split by random seed, and no evaluation seed was used. On held-out seeds its one-step queue error was 1.60 vehicles against 5.23 for a persistence baseline, and it remained below persistence at two and three steps (`models/wm_metrics.json`). Because both inputs and targets are noisy perception outputs, these errors are measured against what the controller observes and not against ground truth, and persistence is a minimum bar and not a strong competitor.
