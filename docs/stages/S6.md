# Stage S6: Slow loop (renderer, VLAM, validator, coordinator, log)

## Purpose and place in the architecture

The AfriCoT-VLA design splits control into a slow loop, in which a vision-language-action model (VLAM) looks at an image of the junction and a short context and proposes a compact subgoal, and a fast loop that plans signal timings against that subgoal (`SPEC.md`, `docs/design.html`). Stage S6 builds the slow loop: a top-down renderer that stands in for a camera keyframe, the VLAM backends, the subgoal validator, the coordinator that decides when the slow loop is called and what happens when it is unreachable, and the per-decision explainability log. The subgoal is the only interface between the two loops, and the shield of the stage on the safety shield still corrects every action that the planner proposes.

## What was built

The code was committed in `7c0d5df`, and the hugging-face smoke output in `c49fb99`.

- `africot/renderer.py`: `snapshot(env)` captures vehicle and pedestrian positions and signal state from SUMO, and `render(snap, path)` draws a 512 by 512 PNG with road outlines, crosswalks, vehicles as shapes coloured by class, waiting pedestrians as dots, the signal state per movement and a legend. The committed example is `examples/snapshot_market_100.png` (market scenario, tuning seed 100, 226 s), with its context in `examples/snapshot_market_100.json`.
- `africot/subgoal.py`: the default subgoal, the permitted ranges, the validator, the response parser and a cached time-of-day schedule.
- `africot/vlam.py`: a deterministic `mock` backend and a `hf` backend based on Hugging Face transformers, with the Qwen2.5-VL family as the default.
- `africot/coordinator.py` and `africot/log.py`: the call schedule, the connectivity logic, the novelty trigger and the explainability log.
- `examples/hf_vlam_smoke.py` and its output `examples/vlam_hf_output.json`.
- `tests/test_slow_loop.py` and `tests/test_subgoal.py`. The dependency `torchvision==0.29.1` was added to `requirements.txt` because the Qwen2-VL processor needs it.

## Exact settings

From `africot/subgoal.py`, `africot/vlam.py` and `africot/coordinator.py`:

- The subgoal holds queue weights for the east, west and south approaches, a pedestrian weight, a maximum wait, a transit priority and a validity period. The default is all queue weights 1.0, pedestrian weight 1.0, maximum wait 120 s, transit priority 1.0 and validity 300 s. Ranges are 0.2 to 3.0 for each queue weight, 0.0 to 3.0 for the pedestrian weight, 45 to 180 s for the maximum wait, 0.0 to 2.0 for transit priority and 60 to 900 s for validity. Out-of-range numbers are clamped. A missing or non-numeric field keeps the previous value, and an unparseable reply keeps the previous subgoal.
- The prompt contains the image, the simulated clock time, the scenario context when known, the current queues, waiting pedestrians and danfos, the current subgoal and the schema with its ranges. The model is asked to reason inside `<reasoning>` tags and to answer with JSON inside `<subgoal>` tags.
- Decoding is greedy (`do_sample=False`) with at most 400 new tokens. The default model is `Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct` in bfloat16 on the CPU, and the model identifier is configurable so that `Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct` can be used on a GPU.
- The coordinator calls the slow loop every 300 simulated seconds and early when the novelty trigger fires. The trigger compares the mean one-step world-model queue error over the last 10 decisions with twice the median of the 30 decisions before them, with at least 120 s between triggers.
- When the scenario marks the slow loop as unreachable, the last subgoal is held, and after more than 600 s offline the cached time-of-day schedule is used. Each connectivity switch is logged. The simulated clock starts at 07:00.
- The slow loop is treated as instantaneous in simulated time, and its wall-clock latency is recorded only.
- Every VLAM record stores the backend, model identifier, raw text, reasoning, parsed and validated subgoal, validator notes, a parse flag and the latency. The mock backend is labelled as intended only for tests and wiring, and every record carries `backend: mock`.

## How it was tested

The reviewer ran `python3.13 -m pytest -q` at the branch head and recorded 68 passed (`reviews/S6.md`). `tests/test_slow_loop.py` covers rendering of a real snapshot to a 512 by 512 PNG, parsing and validation of mock output, retention of the previous subgoal on an unparseable reply, scheduled calls every 300 s, hold-last followed by the cached schedule in an outage, the novelty gap, the incident context appearing only inside the incident window, and an end-to-end mock run. `tests/test_subgoal.py` covers the default, clamping, garbage fields, parsing and coverage of the schedule over the simulated day.

The hugging-face backend was run once on the committed snapshot with the 3B model on a CPU with 15 GB of memory and four cores. The output is in `examples/vlam_hf_output.json`. The reply was parseable (`parse_ok` true), with equal queue weights of 1.0, pedestrian weight 3.0, maximum wait 120 s, transit priority 1.0 and validity 300 s, and no validator notes. The recorded latency was 146.7 s. The reviewer reran the call from a fresh model download and obtained exactly the same raw text and subgoal, which is consistent with deterministic greedy decoding on this CPU. The reviewer's latency was 1198 s because three simulator jobs were sharing the cores, so only the committed figure was measured on an idle machine (`reviews/S6.md`).

The context in that snapshot gives about 11 pedestrians waiting at the east crossing and 2 at the west crossing (`examples/vlam_hf_output.json`). The model's reasoning attributes the 11 waiting pedestrians to the west arm and leaves all queue weights at their defaults, so its chain of thought is not reliable at this model size. The run shows that the pipeline produces a parseable and validated subgoal, and it is not evidence of sound reasoning by the 3B model. No `africot_full` result with a real model is part of this stage.

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The stage was approved without required fixes (`reviews/S6.md`). Three findings are carried into this document. The 3B model's reasoning is unreliable, as described above (informational). One call takes about 150 s on an idle four-core CPU while the simulated slow loop is treated as instantaneous (informational, stated under limitations). The field `valid_for_s` is validated but not used by the coordinator, which calls on its own fixed schedule (low, stated under limitations).

## Limitations

The slow loop is idealised as instantaneous, so the roughly 150 s latency of the 3B model on the CPU has no effect on simulated control. The validity field of the subgoal has no effect on the schedule. The renderer draws a simulator view and is not a camera image, so any benefit of visual reasoning on real footage is untested. The only real-model output so far is a single call on one snapshot with the 3B model, which cannot support a claim about the quality of the strategic reasoning, and the 7B grid is left to the Colab notebook of the later tooling stage. The cached time-of-day schedule is a hand-written design choice and was not tuned.

## Dissertation paragraph

The slow loop is designed to translate a top-down image of the junction and a short textual context into a compact subgoal that weights the objectives of the fast planner. A renderer produces a 512 by 512 keyframe, a vision-language-action model replies with a reasoning trace and a JSON subgoal, and a validator clamps every value to a permitted range and keeps the previous subgoal when a reply cannot be parsed. A coordinator is intended to call the model every 300 simulated seconds or when the world model signals novelty, to hold the last subgoal during connectivity loss, and to fall back to a cached time-of-day schedule after ten minutes offline. A single call with the 3B Qwen2.5-VL model produced a parseable, validated subgoal, but its reasoning misattributed the waiting pedestrians to the wrong arm (`examples/vlam_hf_output.json`), so this output is used only to verify the pipeline and not as evidence of reliable reasoning.
