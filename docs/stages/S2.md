# Stage S2: Perception, metrics and baselines

## Purpose and place in the architecture

In the AfriCoT-VLA architecture (`SPEC.md`, `docs/design.html`) every controller observes the junction only through a camera-like perception layer, and every controller is compared on the same metrics against conventional baselines. Stage S2 builds that common layer: a noisy perception model, the performance metrics, three baseline controllers (fixed_time, count_timer and max_pressure) and the experiment runner. The stage is intended to give the later fast-loop and slow-loop controllers a fair reference, and to ensure that no controller uses simulator ground truth for control.

## What was built

The first version was committed in `a80237e` with results in `a55e7fa`. After review, all 60 baseline results were regenerated in `c867954` from code commit `91dca21`.

- `africot/perception.py`, the noisy perception model.
- `africot/baselines.py`, the three baseline controllers.
- `africot/metrics.py`, the metrics (shared with S1).
- `run_experiment.py` and `run_grid.py`, which run one controller on one scenario and seed and write one JSON file per run to `results/`.
- Tests in `tests/test_perception.py` and `tests/test_baselines.py`.

## Exact settings

Perception (`africot/perception.py`). For each approach the model reports the queue, counts by class, halting vehicles by class, occupancy and departures since the previous observation. It also reports waiting pedestrians per crossing and halting vehicles per movement. Each vehicle or pedestrian is missed with probability 0.05 (binomial thinning), Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 10% of the value (minimum 0.5) is added, and the result is clipped at zero. The generator is seeded per run. Fields that are ground truth only (`max_wait`, `ped_max_wait`, `teleports`) never reach a controller, which `tests/test_perception.py` checks.

Baselines (`africot/baselines.py`):

- fixed_time: cycle A 35 s, B 12 s, C 25 s.
- count_timer: each green starts at 10 s and is extended in 5 s steps while the observed queue on the movements served with right of way is at least 3, up to 60 s. Phases cycle A, B, C.
- max_pressure: selects the phase whose served movements have the largest observed queue, with the downstream treated as empty, using a fixed 15 s green. Ties keep the current phase.

Runs use evaluation seeds 0 to 4 on all four scenarios, which gives 60 runs (3 controllers, 4 scenarios, 5 seeds), each over the 3900 s horizon with the 300 s warm-up excluded. Each result file records `seed_role: evaluation` and `vlam_backend: null`.

## How it was tested

The builder's note records 32 tests passing at the first build (`STATUS.md`). The reviewer's rerun at the later branch head recorded 49 passed (`reviews/S2.md`, re-review). The tests check that perception is seeded and reproducible, that its noise statistics match the stated parameters, that ground-truth fields do not leak, that clean mode returns the truth, that departures are per interval, the fixed_time cycle, the count_timer extension and cap, max_pressure phase selection, and the metric handling of warm-up and insertion delay.

Mean results over evaluation seeds 0 to 4, computed from the 60 files in `results/` (columns are AWT in s, ATT in s, throughput in vehicles):

| scenario | fixed_time | count_timer | max_pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| normal | 36.3 / 100.8 / 1698 | 38.1 / 104.0 / 1704 | 17.0 / 76.5 / 1715 |
| market | 42.4 / 109.4 / 1597 | 44.1 / 111.5 / 1601 | 25.9 / 88.7 / 1598 |
| incident | 56.8 / 132.9 / 1697 | 52.0 / 125.6 / 1702 | 35.8 / 108.3 / 1713 |
| outage | 42.4 / 109.4 / 1597 | 44.1 / 111.5 / 1601 | 25.9 / 88.7 / 1598 |

The stage condition was that max_pressure AWT is lower than fixed_time on the normal scenario, and it holds (17.0 s against 36.3 s). The outage rows equal the market rows because the baselines have no slow loop that the connectivity outage could affect. In the files, at most 2 vehicles per run were never inserted (the maximum over all 60 runs; for example `results/fixed_time__normal__4.json`). One jam teleport occurred over the 60 runs, in max_pressure on market and outage seed 3.

The reviewer reran four runs from scratch (fixed_time market 0, max_pressure normal 3, count_timer incident 2, max_pressure market 1) and found the full metrics dictionaries identical to the committed files (`reviews/S2.md`).

## Deviations from the specification

From `DEVIATIONS.md`:

- Perception also returns halting vehicles per movement (`movement_queues`), derived from each halting vehicle's route and with the same noise as the other counts. The specification lists per-approach quantities, but max_pressure needs per-movement queues to distinguish phase A from phase B.
- ATT and AWT include insertion delay and censor at 3900 s (see the S1 write-up in `docs/stages/S1.md`), and AQL counts halting demand vehicles only.

## Reviewer findings and resolution

The first review requested changes (`reviews/S2.md`). The market ranking, in which both adaptive baselines appeared worse than fixed_time, was an artefact of metrics that excluded insertion delay (finding 1). The market scenario was gridlocked (finding 2), and the recorded `git_hash` did not exist in the repository (finding 3). Two minor items concerned AQL counting the incident blocker (finding 4) and an undocumented per-movement queue deviation (finding 5, since added to `DEVIATIONS.md`). All 60 runs were regenerated after the S1 fixes and the re-review approved the stage.

Residual notes from the re-review: 59 of 60 files record the code hash `91dca21c21a6289b9e9a8ec1fd2b7004df189027` with a `-dirty` suffix, because the grid overwrites tracked result files before the later runs check the working tree (`run_experiment.py`). The reviewer found that the code at that commit reproduces the files exactly, so this is a labelling artefact. The reviewer also corrected the builder's note: the maximum number of never-inserted vehicles is 2, not 1.

## Limitations

Perception is a statistical stand-in for camera detection and does not model occlusion, lighting or class confusion. The per-movement queue reading assumes that turn intent is visible. The baselines are simple and untuned beyond their stated parameters; max_pressure treats the downstream as empty because the junction is isolated. Because outage equals market for these baselines, the outage scenario only becomes informative once the slow loop exists. Results use five evaluation seeds, so the means carry sampling variability that this stage does not quantify. The one jam teleport in max_pressure (market and outage seed 3) arises from vehicles held inside the junction while yielding to dense pedestrian flow, as noted in `reviews/S3.md`.

## Text for the dissertation

Controllers are designed to observe the junction only through a seeded noisy perception model, in which each vehicle or pedestrian is missed with probability 0.05 and Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 10% of the count is added. Three conventional baselines provide the reference: a fixed-time plan of 35, 12 and 25 s, a count-and-timer actuated controller, and a max-pressure controller with a 15 s green. All were run on the four scenarios with five evaluation seeds. Over these seeds, max_pressure had the lowest mean average waiting time in every scenario, for example 17.0 s against 36.3 s for fixed_time in the normal scenario and 25.9 s against 42.4 s in the market scenario (`results/*.json`, aggregated over files named by controller, scenario and seed). Delay metrics include insertion delay, which was introduced after an independent review found that a plain reading of SUMO's trip statistics let a controller that starved an approach appear to perform better.
