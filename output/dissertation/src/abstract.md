Traffic signal control in Sub-Saharan African cities must cope with mixed informal transit, intermittent connectivity and power, and a need for auditable decisions. This dissertation proposes AfriCoT-VLA, a cloud-based traffic control system, with cloud-hosted reasoning and edge fallback for intermittent connectivity, following a supervisor-proposed eight-layer framework. In the intended deployment, a cloud-hosted vision-language-action model with chain-of-thought reasoning turns a compressed junction keyframe and its context, every few minutes, into a compact subgoal with a written justification. An on-site latent world model and a cross-entropy method planner choose phase timings against that subgoal, behind a rule-based safety shield that nothing learned can bypass, and keep the junction running when the link drops. A T-junction with cars, minibuses, tricycles and motorcycles was built in SUMO and examined in four scenarios over five seeds, against fixed-time, camera-count-plus-timer and max-pressure baselines. On held-out data the world model lowered one-step queue prediction error by {{world_model.test.1step.queue_improvement_vs_persistence::pct1}} percent compared with a persistence guess. The planner without the slow loop lowered average waiting time against fixed-time on all three scenarios, but max-pressure was lower on normal in all five seeds, and the two were comparable on market and incident. Without the world model it was no better than fixed-time. The shield recorded {{safety_violations_total::0}} violations in evaluation. <!--COLAB:abstract_status-->The full system, including the slow loop, is implemented and wiring-tested with a scripted backend and one smoke test of a 3-billion-parameter model, but it has not yet been evaluated, so no claim is made about its benefit.<!--/COLAB--> No cloud deployment has been built or measured, so no latency, cost, availability, security or data-residency result exists. The prototype is limited to one junction, simulated perception and five seeds, and two planned baselines are not built.
