# Chapter 5: Summary, Conclusion, Recommendations and Future Work

## 5.1 Summary of the Study

This dissertation set out to examine whether a traffic signal controller designed around Sub-Saharan African constraints could be specified, built and tested in simulation. The design couples two models only through a compact subgoal. A slow loop, intended to be served by a vision-language-action model with Chain-of-Thought reasoning, reads keyframes and context and proposes cost weights and constraint bounds. A fast loop uses a latent world model and a Cross-Entropy Method planner to choose phase timings on site. A rule-based safety shield sits between the planner and the signals, and every decision is intended to be written to an explainability log. The eight-layer deployment framework, proposed by Dr. Fasina during the class session of 27 July 2026, was kept as the organising structure, with the cloud treated as the full-connectivity tier and Layer 2 carrying compressed keyframes for the slow loop rather than continuous video. The refinements to individual layers remain the author's proposals and have not been confirmed by the supervisor.

The prototype was built for a single Lagos-style T-junction in SUMO, controlled through TraCI, with four scenarios (normal peak, market day, incident and network outage), five evaluation seeds per cell, and {{n_eval_runs::0}} reportable runs in total. Perception is SUMO ground truth with noise, so the detector itself was not tested. Five controllers were evaluated: fixed-time, a camera-count-plus-timer rule, max-pressure, and two ablations of the proposed system, one without the slow loop (`africot_noslow`) and one without the world model (`africot_nowm`). The full controller, `africot_full`, is implemented and wiring-tested with a rule-based mock backend, and the vision-language model was smoke-tested once with Qwen2.5-VL-3B on a CPU, but it has not been evaluated.

The main findings are as follows. The latent world model, trained on data that excluded the evaluation seeds, reached a one-step queue error of {{world_model.test.1step.queue_mae_model::2}} vehicles on held-out seeds against {{world_model.test.1step.queue_mae_persistence::2}} for a persistence baseline. The safety shield recorded {{safety_violations_total::0}} violations across all {{n_eval_runs::0}} runs, as judged by an independent ground-truth audit. On the normal scenario, `africot_noslow` reached a mean average waiting time (AWT) of {{summary.normal|africot_noslow.awt_mean::1}} s, against {{summary.normal|fixed_time.awt_mean::1}} s for fixed-time but {{summary.normal|max_pressure.awt_mean::1}} s for max-pressure, which was lower in all five seeds. The same ordering holds on the incident scenario ({{summary.incident|africot_noslow.awt_mean::1}} s against {{summary.incident|max_pressure.awt_mean::1}} s). On the market scenario the two are comparable ({{summary.market|africot_noslow.awt_mean::1}} s against {{summary.market|max_pressure.awt_mean::1}} s, with a standard deviation of {{summary.market|max_pressure.awt_sd::1}} s for max-pressure), and the paired test is not significant. ## 5.2 Conclusions

The evidence supports four conclusions, each limited by the simulation-only scope. First, a small latent world model trained on perception-level queue, pedestrian and departure data appears able to predict short-horizon queue evolution on this junction considerably better than assuming no change. This supports using it as the planner's internal model, but it was tested only on one junction and on simulated perception.

Second, the world model appears to matter for the planner, and a model-predictive fast loop is lower in waiting than fixed-time on every scenario in the means. It was not better than max-pressure on the normal and incident scenarios, and the evidence does not show that it is better than max-pressure anywhere. The design is therefore best described as competitive with a strong classical baseline on one scenario and behind it on two. The `africot_nowm` result also shows that the planner by itself is not the source of any benefit: without the world model it was no improvement on fixed-time.

Third, the safety shield behaved as intended. No unsafe timing was recorded in the audited runs, and nearly all corrections concerned pedestrian crossing time ({{shield_total_by_rule.ped_crossing::0}} corrections, against {{shield_total_by_rule.max_green::0}} for maximum green and {{shield_total_by_rule.starvation::0}} for starvation). This supports placing learned or heuristic proposals behind a rule layer that they cannot bypass, though the audit shares the simulator's assumptions about conflicts.

Fourth, the evidence on the slow loop is limited to engineering readiness. The mock-backend runs showed that scheduled calls, the novelty trigger, the hold-last and cached-schedule fallbacks under outage, and the log fields work as wired. The novelty trigger is not specific to the incident, since it also fired in the normal, market and outage scenarios. The 3B smoke test produced a parseable subgoal, but its reasoning attributed the waiting pedestrians to the wrong arm, so Chain-of-Thought from a model of that size is unreliable. Outage is also not an independent scenario for any controller except `africot_full`, because the results for the others equal their market results.

## 5.3 Contributions

The contributions are modest and should be read as such. The work offers a concrete specification of a two-timescale, coupled (not fused) controller that treats connectivity loss, limited sensing and auditability as design inputs; a reproducible SUMO junction with informal transit classes, motorcycle lane-splitting and four scenarios; a rule-based shield with an independent audit; a world model and planner with ablations; and the full set of results, including those that went against the design. The negative results are part of the contribution, because they indicate where the design needs work before any claim about the slow loop is justified.

## 5.4 Recommendations

Because the evidence does not show an advantage over max-pressure, practitioners should treat max-pressure behind the shield as the reference controller for any comparable setting, and should regard the present design as a research prototype. Any adaptive controller intended for deployment in Lagos should keep a rule-based shield that no learned component can bypass, a fixed-time fallback, and a decision log suitable for audit by LASTMA and the Ministry of Transportation. Researchers evaluating signal controllers under informal traffic should report censored delay that includes vehicles never inserted, since otherwise a controller that starves a side road can appear better than it is, and should report paired by-seed results rather than relying on means that differ by less than one standard deviation.

## 5.5 Limitations

The study covers one junction, simulated perception and five seeds per cell, and the market side-road demand had to be lowered from 550 to 200 vehicles per hour to avoid gridlock. The deep reinforcement learning and LLMLight baselines named in the plan were not built. The paired tests are exploratory, with five seeds and no correction for multiple comparisons. No physical field trial was attempted.

## 5.6 Future Work

The immediate step is the GPU evaluation of `africot_full` with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct through the Colab notebook, which writes results and logs to Drive, is resumable, and records the backend and model in every result file. The two missing baselines, a deep reinforcement learning controller and LLMLight, should then be added so that the comparison includes the learned and language-model methods named in the plan.

The next extension is a corridor of two to three junctions with neighbour exchange, in which each world model takes its neighbours' outflows as inputs and queue and plan data are shared, so that the effect of neighbour information and of the mesh relay under outage can be tested directly. Capability-aware routing and the tiered communication scheme should be compared with fixed routing in that setting. The perception module should be tested separately on real Lagos footage, since the present noise model is only a stand-in.

The deployment roadmap described in this work, but not built, proceeds in stages: hardware-in-the-loop testing on Jetson-class edge hardware, then shadow mode, in which the system proposes but does not act, then a supervised pilot with operator override, and finally live operation. A timeline cannot yet be given, because the submission and defence date is not yet known. It is to be added once that date is fixed.
